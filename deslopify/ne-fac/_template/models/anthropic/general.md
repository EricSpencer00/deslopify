## Use concrete details instead of recurring weight metaphors (`anthropic-concrete-fiction-details`)

**Writing · Don’t · Company: anthropic**

**When:** Fiction

Do not substitute recurring weight metaphors for a concrete description.

**Example — follows the rule:**

Her fingers tightened around the unsigned letter.

**Counterexample — breaks the rule:**

The weight of the moment weighed heavily with the weight of memory.

**Why:** Scene-specific actions communicate more than repeated metaphors.

## Do not invent the author’s experiences (`anthropic-dont-invent-experiences`)

**Writing · Don’t · Company: anthropic**

**When:** Any writing

Do not invent experiences for the author.

**Example — follows the rule:**

Write “The report describes a difficult launch” when only the report is supplied.

**Counterexample — breaks the rule:**

Write “I remember losing sleep during that launch” for an author who never supplied that experience.

**Why:** The writer’s personal history must come from the writer.

## Keep technical explanations plain (`anthropic-plain-technical-language`)

**Writing · Don’t · Company: anthropic**

**When:** Paper writing

Do not turn a plain technical explanation into poetic jargon.

**Example — follows the rule:**

The cache reuses results from earlier requests.

**Counterexample — breaks the rule:**

**Why:** Concrete language explains the mechanism.
