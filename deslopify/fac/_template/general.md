## Make overview pages answer reader questions (`about-pages-answer-questions`)

**Writing · Do · All models**

**When:** Public-facing frontend copy

About and overview pages should answer a reader's actual questions, not narrate the navigation. Skip repeated brand-name heroes, vague mission/tagline filler, and tours of every section; keep concrete identity, ownership, contact, and policy facts where they help the reader.

**Example — follows the rule:**

About / Employee-owned design studio. / Contact hello@example.org.

**Counterexample — breaks the rule:**

Studio / About / A creative home for meaningful experiences. / Browse the projects and learn about the team.

**Why:** An overview should provide concrete identity and useful facts.

## Let the argument set the length (`argument-led-length`)

**Writing · Do · All models**

**When:** Paper writing

## Keep details that matter to the audience (`audience-relevant-details`)

**Writing · Do · All models**

**When:** Any reader-facing writing

**DO NOT say things that do not matter to the audience.**

Keep a detail only when it helps the reader understand the subject, make a decision, or take the intended action. A fact being true or available is not a reason to include it. Cut internal bookkeeping, process narration, unrelated history, and implementation details unless the audience needs them. Keep qualifications that materially affect the reader's decision; state them once, plainly.

**Example — follows the rule:**

The letter gives the applicant’s relevant experience and the requested next step.

**Counterexample — breaks the rule:**

The letter narrates internal draft filenames and revision counters.

**Why:** Readers need details that support understanding, decisions, or action.

## Preserve useful and accessible UI copy (`preserve-needed-ui-copy`)

**Design · Do · All models**

**When:** Public-facing frontend copy

Keep copy that supplies needed context or navigation, accessible names and alt text, instructions, errors, product facts, and legal disclosures. A useful heading or label earns its place; minimalism is not a reason to remove it.

**Example — follows the rule:**

**Counterexample — breaks the rule:**
